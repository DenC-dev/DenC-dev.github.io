Where do Idaté and Lakalei belong? A pre-registered lexical-phylogenetic test of two classifications of Timor’s Austronesian languages

Liabut. Phaistos Labs working paper, version 4, November 2026

paper / supplement / website / code and data / how to reproduce / release checks / cognate benchmark / classification sensitivity / replication checklist

Researchers disagree about how Idaté and Lakalei, two small languages of Timor-Leste, are related to other languages on the island. I compared word lists from across Timor, but the evidence does not point to one clear answer: the two languages group with different language families in different analyses. The project also creates resources that other researchers can use: a digital collection of 14,401 words from 257 sources; a comparison showing that computer-extracted words agree with expert review 94% of the time; an expert-coded benchmark where automatic cognate detection scores an F1 of 0.81; descriptions of sound changes across 65 languages; and a review of 740 Tetun words that resemble words in other languages, identifying 21 likely borrowings. These materials can help researchers compare languages, spot borrowed words and test new language-analysis tools.

Recovering Timucua: A Computer-Assisted Framework for Text Analysis and Contact Detection in Mikasuki and Muscogee

Phaistos Labs research proposal and pilot database, 2026

website / database / CLDF / SQLite / release checks / calibration audit / archival text annotation tool / next calibration design

Timucua was spoken in northern Florida until the 1700s. I’m studying whether it left words in Mikasuki or Muscogee, languages spoken by the Seminole and Miccosukee. So far, comparisons with Mikasuki, Creek and Apalachee word lists have not found convincing evidence of a large number of Timucua words. Three unusual Mikasuki words—“who,” “tree” and “fire”—also appear in records of Hitchiti from the 1880s, suggesting they were in use before the Seminole moved to Florida. The project has produced a searchable collection of 6,799 words from 67 languages, with 2,424 judgments about which words share a common origin, 32 tests for possible language contact and a list of 24 place names. The collection gives other researchers a way to check the findings and test new methods for tracing how languages influence one another. A calibration audit also shows that the current test misses some known contact cases, so it cannot rule out small or individual Timucua borrowings.

Reading the Khitan small script

Early stage, 2026

starting point: Kane, The Kitan Language and Script (2009)

The Khitan small script was used during the Liao dynasty (916–1125) in northern China, and only part of it can be read today. Its language was probably a distant relative of Mongolian. I’m just getting started, working from Kane’s readings and seeing how much further NLP methods, Mongolic cognates and the Chinese texts written alongside Khitan ones can take them.
